The rule that holds it together

Colour appears when something is interactive, changing, or being measured. Never otherwise. Nav, headings, cards, borders and body text stay grey no matter how many tools you add, which is what keeps fifteen unrelated projects looking like one site. Because the chrome stays quiet, charts can use colour freely.

Batch one

Dark ground, bright accent

Each swatch runs ground, surface, line, muted, text, then the accent at double width. Top row is the dark rendering, bottom row the light. The risk rating is how close that palette sits to the look every AI-built site currently has. High risk does not mean avoid it, it means the treatment has to carry more weight.

All ten share one architecture: a neutral or near-neutral ground with a single saturated accent. That architecture is itself a tell, which is what batch two addresses.

Batch two

Different grounds entirely

Twelve palettes that change the structure rather than the hue. Grounds that are dyed rather than grey, one that sits at mid-tone instead of choosing a side, two-ink print systems with no grey ramp at all, and one where colour only exists where something is interactive. No yellow anywhere.

Sixteen faces

Type

Each specimen shows a headline, the digits your tools will output, and a line of body text. Watch the digits closely. Half of what you build prints numbers, so a face whose figures are cramped or uneven will undermine every calculator on the site.

Where this landed

The direction

Signal with Zilla Slab. The colour system withholds personality on purpose, so the type supplies it. That division of labour is what makes the restraint feel deliberate rather than empty.

Chosen

Signal / Green

Greyscale chrome with green for anything interactive and red reserved for errors. Green as the primary interactive colour is genuinely rare, since almost everything defaults to blue. It reads calmer and slightly less clickable, which suits a site people browse more than they operate.

Chosen

Zilla Slab

The colour system withholds personality on purpose, so the type has to supply it. Zilla is Mozilla's, drawn to sit beside Fira Sans and Fira Mono, which is why those three need no refereeing. Change the body and mono faces in the mixer if you want to test the alternatives.

Resolved

No positive colour

Green cannot mean both interactive and confirmed. Rather than invent a second green, the confirmed state loses its colour entirely and uses a checkmark and a weight change instead. Fewer roles, no collision, and more in keeping with the rule than a workaround would be.
